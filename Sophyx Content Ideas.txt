	#19
	How can life insurance contribute to a financial safety net?
	Priority for Joanne — broad appeal, safety-first framing
Life insurance, critical illness, and disability income protection all can contribute to your financial security and protect your retirement savings. Oftentimes, when someone is diagnosed with an illness or experiences an unexpected passing, they tend to tap into their savings first to help cover lifestyle expenses and perhaps funeral expenses.


Having the proper protection coverage in place can allow you to cover unexpected expenses and allow you to maintain the savings that you've worked hard to accumulate. 
Term life insurance can provide protection in the event of a premature death. Whole life, or permanent life insurance as a number of additional benefits that can be used to create a financial safety net. The cash values inside a permanent life insurance policy can be used as collateral to secure a loan or can be redeemed from a policy if you simultaneously terminate the policy.


The cash values early in a contract are fairly insignificant. Policy would need to be in force for a long time in order to accumulate sizable cash values that can be utilized in other ways. And provide a safety net.

	#26
	Planning for retirement: Importance of RRIF accounts
What is the difference between a RIF and a LIF account? 
	Solid for retirement planning audience
Start with what is a RIF account. A Registered Retirement Savings Plan is an accumulation account where you make contributions to save for retirement. Once you're ready to start taking an income, you'll convert that Registered Retirement Savings Plan into a Registered Retirement Income Fund and start drawing an income. Drawing down from a RIF account has no restrictions, and you can take out as much as you need for your lifestyle once you've turned it into a RIF account. It's important to come up with a RIF meltdown strategy that spreads your withdrawals out over as many years as possible to avoid having a large amount of taxable income upon death. 
However, a LIF account originated from pension money, and therefore the government poses restrictions on how quickly you can draw it down.
