Most people think of their savings as their financial safety net: the account they'll draw from if something goes wrong. But what happens when a serious illness or an unexpected death is the very thing that causes them to drain it? That's the gap that protection insurance is designed to close.

Life insurance, critical illness, and disability income protection all can contribute to your financial security and protect your retirement savings. Oftentimes, when someone is diagnosed with an illness or experiences an unexpected passing, they tend to tap into their savings first to help cover lifestyle expenses and perhaps funeral expenses.

Having the proper protection coverage in place can allow you to cover unexpected expenses and allow you to maintain the savings that you've worked hard to accumulate. That's what a real financial safety net looks like: not just the savings themselves, but the protection that keeps those savings intact when life doesn't go to plan.

What is a financial safety net?

A financial safety net is a combination of savings, income, and insurance that keeps you financially stable during an unexpected event: illness, disability, or death. Protection insurance is the layer that prevents a life crisis from becoming a financial one.

Three Types of Protection That Contribute to Your Safety Net

Protection insurance isn't a single product; it's a category of coverage that addresses different risks. Here's how each type works and what it protects.

1

Term Life Insurance

Term life insurance can provide protection in the event of a premature death. It's designed to cover a temporary need: a mortgage, a loan, or family income replacement at a time in your life when you have fewer liquid assets to fall back on. If you pass away during the term, a tax-free benefit is paid to your beneficiaries, so they aren't left managing debt or lifestyle expenses out of their own savings.

Term insurance is typically the most affordable entry point into life insurance, which makes it a practical first step for younger families building their financial foundation.

2

Permanent (Whole) Life Insurance

Whole life, or permanent life insurance, has a number of additional benefits that can be used to create a financial safety net beyond the death benefit alone. Unlike term insurance, permanent coverage doesn't expire; it remains in force for your lifetime and builds cash value over time that can serve multiple roles in your financial plan.

Permanent life insurance is designed for building wealth, creating estate solutions, and supporting legacy goals for clients who want to leave something meaningful to the people and causes they care about.

3

Critical Illness & Disability Income Protection

Critical illness and disability income protection address the risks that life insurance doesn't cover. Specifically, what happens if you become seriously ill or are unable to work, but don't pass away. These are living benefits, meaning they pay out while you're still here and still have expenses to manage.

Why living benefits matter for your savings

A cancer diagnosis, heart attack, or long-term disability can create immediate financial pressure. Without coverage, the instinct is to tap into savings: retirement accounts, investments, and home equity, drawn on to cover lifestyle expenses while income is interrupted. The right living benefits coverage means those savings stay intact.

How Permanent Life Insurance Builds Cash Value Over Time

One of the features that sets permanent life insurance apart is its ability to accumulate cash value inside the policy. This feature can contribute to your financial safety net in meaningful ways over the long term.

The cash values inside a permanent life insurance policy can be used as collateral to secure a loan, or can be redeemed from the policy if you simultaneously terminate the policy. This gives policyholders a source of accessible capital that doesn't require selling an investment or triggering a taxable event in the same way a withdrawal might.

💡 The cash values early in a contract are fairly insignificant. A policy would need to be in force for a long time in order to accumulate sizable cash values that can be utilized in other ways and provide a meaningful safety net.

This is an important distinction for anyone considering a permanent policy primarily for its cash value: this is a long-game strategy. The benefit compounds over decades, not years, which is why permanent life insurance works best for clients thinking about legacy, estate planning, or long-term wealth alongside the protection benefit.

Permanent life insurance is not a short-term savings vehicle; it's a long-term wealth and protection strategy. The longer the policy is in force, the more its cash value can contribute to your overall financial picture.

Term vs. Permanent Life Insurance: Understanding the Difference

Choosing between term and permanent life insurance comes down to what you need protection against and for how long. Here's a side-by-side comparison to help clarify where each one fits.

Term vs. Permanent Life Insurance: At a Glance
FeatureTerm Life InsurancePermanent Life Insurance
Coverage period Fixed term (e.g., 10, 20, 30 years) Lifetime
Primary purpose Income replacement, mortgage, family protection Wealth building, legacy, estate planning
Builds cash value No Yes, grows over time
Premium cost Lower Higher, reflecting lifelong coverage
Best suited for Earlier life stages with fewer liquid assets Long-term wealth and protection strategy
Contributes to safety net by Replacing income and covering debts on death Death benefit + accessible cash value over time

Many Canadians benefit from both types at different stages of life. A term policy may make sense early on to cover immediate obligations, while a permanent policy becomes part of a longer-term wealth and protection strategy as your financial picture evolves.

Protection Insurance as Part of a Complete Financial Plan

Life insurance and living benefits aren't separate from your financial plan; they're part of it. Investments and insurance are complementary vehicles used to create a holistic and comprehensive financial plan. The savings and investments you've worked hard to build are worth protecting, and protection coverage is how you do that.

Without the right coverage in place, a diagnosis, a disability, or an unexpected death can force you to access retirement savings early, pay unnecessary tax, or liquidate investments at the wrong time. With the right coverage, those savings stay untouched and continue working for you.
